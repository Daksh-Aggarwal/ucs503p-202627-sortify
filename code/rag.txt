# Sortify Comprehensive Waste Knowledge Base (RAG Reference Document)
# Format: [Item Name / Topic] followed by Category, Disposal Instructions, Material & Recycling Context, Caution, and Regional Notes.
# Rule: No emojis anywhere in this document.

[Plastic Water Bottles & PET Beverage Containers]
Category: Recyclable
Disposal Instructions:
- Empty all remaining liquids completely and rinse briefly to prevent contamination.
- Keep the plastic cap screwed onto the bottle to ensure both cap and bottle are processed together at the recovery facility.
- Flatten the bottle gently to conserve volume in transport and storage.
- Place directly into the yellow-lid or blue commingled recycling bin loose, never inside a tied plastic bag.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Made of Polyethylene Terephthalate (resin code 1).
- PET is shredded into flakes, washed, and pelletized into food-grade rPET pellets for new bottles, polyester textiles, and fleece jackets.
Caution:
- Bottles that previously stored motor oils, lawn pesticides, or toxic solvents cannot be recycled curbside and must go to household chemical cleanup events.
Regional Notes:
- Eligible beverage bottles can be returned through Container Deposit Scheme (CDS) refund kiosks for a 10-cent refund where available.

[HDPE Milk Bottles, Shampoo Jugs & Detergent Containers]
Category: Recyclable
Disposal Instructions:
- Empty residue thoroughly and rinse with warm water.
- Remove pump dispensers that contain internal metal springs, as mixed-metal pumps jam granulators.
- Place the HDPE bottle into curbside recycling loose.
Material & Recycling Context:
- High-Density Polyethylene (resin code 2) offers high tensile strength and chemical resistance.
- Recycled HDPE is reprocessed into plastic lumber, outdoor playground equipment, agricultural drainage pipes, and non-food detergent jugs.
Caution:
- Do not mix motor oil containers with dairy jugs even if both are marked HDPE; oil penetrates plastic pores.
Regional Notes:
- Standard curbside collection accepts all clear and colored HDPE bottles.

[PVC Pipes, Rigid Plastic Tubing & Vinyl Packaging]
Category: General waste / Construction Drop-off
Disposal Instructions:
- Do not place rigid PVC or vinyl packaging into domestic yellow recycling bins.
- For small domestic offcuts, place in the general waste red-lid bin.
- For large construction pipes and vinyl siding, transport to specialized construction and demolition resource recovery centers.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Polyvinyl Chloride (resin code 3) contains high chlorine content and plasticizers.
- Even minor PVC contamination in a batch of PET or HDPE produces corrosive hydrochloric acid gas and degrades entire recycled resin batches.
Caution:
- Never burn PVC waste under any circumstances, as combustion emits hazardous dioxins and acidic vapors.
Regional Notes:
- Some plumbing trade suppliers offer PVC take-back bins for clean unglued pipe cutoffs.

[LDPE Plastic Bags, Bubble Wrap & Squeeze Bottles]
Category: General waste / Specialized Store Drop-off
Disposal Instructions:
- Keep all flexible soft plastics out of curbside yellow bins.
- Collect clean, dry bread bags, bubble wrap, produce bags, and dry cleaning film into a dedicated container.
- Bring collected soft plastics to dedicated commercial supermarket return drop-offs where such programs operate.
- If no dedicated soft plastic drop-off is accessible in your municipality, discard with general rubbish.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Low-Density Polyethylene (resin code 4) is lightweight and flexible.
- When placed in standard automated sorting machines, film plastics wrap around rotating screens and conveyors, forcing facility shutdowns.
Caution:
- Wet or food-soiled plastic films spoil soft plastic recycling and should be placed in red-lid rubbish.
Regional Notes:
- Never tie recyclables inside a plastic garbage bag before throwing them in the recycling bin.

[PP Polypropylene Yogurt Tubs, Takeaway Containers & Bottle Caps]
Category: Recyclable
Disposal Instructions:
- Scrape away lingering food sauces and dairy solids, rinsing briefly.
- Replace clean PP lids onto matching tubs so they do not fall through sorting grates.
- Deposit clean containers loose into your commingled curbside recycling bin.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Polypropylene (resin code 5) has high thermal tolerance and flexural fatigue resistance.
- Reprocessed PP pellets are manufactured into automotive battery cases, toolboxes, compost bins, storage totes, and broom bristles.
Caution:
- Avoid placing black plastic PP takeaway containers into single-stream recycling unless optical sorters in your area use black-spectrum infrared sensors.
Regional Notes:
- Accepted in almost all metropolitan municipal recycling services across Australia and Europe.

[Polystyrene Foam, Styrofoam Packaging & Foam Peanuts]
Category: General waste / Specialized Drop-off
Disposal Instructions:
- Keep all expanded polystyrene (EPS) foam blocks, meat trays, and foam cups out of curbside recycling bins.
- Break large packaging blocks from electronic appliances and check if your local council transfer station has an EPS densifier machine.
- If transfer drop-off is unavailable, break down foam blocks and place in the general waste red-lid bin.
- For packaging foam peanuts, reuse for mailing or verify if they are starch-based (dissolves quickly under tap water) to dissolve or compost.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Polystyrene (resin code 6) consists of 95% to 98% air.
- Standard sorting machinery fragments EPS into thousands of static beads that contaminate all other material streams.
Caution:
- Foam is combustible and easily windblown into stormwater drains and marine habitats.
Regional Notes:
- Many civic waste management depots accept clean, white molded appliance foam for free.

[Other Mixed Plastics, Multi-layer Pouches & PLA Bioplastics]
Category: General waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Items stamped with resin code 7 (Other) or made of multiple laminated foil-plastic layers must go into the general waste bin.
- Squeezy condiment pouches, metallized chip packets, and composite coffee bags belong in general waste.
- PLA (polylactic acid) bio-plastics must not be put into plastic recycling or standard home compost.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Category 7 includes mixed resins, polycarbonate, fiberglass, and plant-derived polymers that cannot be melted down with common commodity plastics.
- Mixing PLA into a batch of PET degrades the tensile strength of the recycled resin.
Caution:
- Compostable bioplastics require commercial composting heat levels exceeding 55 degrees Celsius to decompose.
Regional Notes:
- Dispose in municipal red-lid general waste unless an industrial composting service explicitly collects PLA.

[Cardboard Boxes & Corrugated Packaging]
Category: Recyclable / Compostable
Disposal Instructions:
- Flatten all corrugated cardboard boxes completely to conserve space in collection trucks.
- Strip away plastic packing tape, styrofoam inserts, and clear plastic shipping sleeves.
- Keep cardboard dry; damp cardboard weakens paper pulp fibers and harbors mold.
- Place flattened clean boxes in your curbside paper and cardboard recycling bin.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Cardboard is composed of strong, unbleached kraft cellulose fibers.
- Cellulose can be repulped up to 5 to 7 times into new shipping boxes, egg cartons, and paperboard packaging.
Caution:
- Cardboard soaked in motor oil, paints, or thick animal grease cannot be repulped and must be placed in general waste.
Regional Notes:
- Clean shredded plain cardboard without glossy ink can also serve as brown carbon material in home backyard compost piles.

[Greasy Pizza Boxes & Oil-Contaminated Paperboard]
Category: Organic / Compost / General waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Inspect the pizza box: separate the clean cardboard top from the grease-soaked bottom.
- Tear off the clean top half and place it with paper and cardboard recycling.
- If your municipality has a green-lid FOGO (Food Organics and Garden Organics) service that accepts food-soiled paper, place the greasy bottom into the green bin.
- If FOGO is not available or does not allow paperboard, discard the greasy bottom into the general waste red-lid bin.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Vegetable oils and cheese grease adhere tightly to paper fibers.
- In paper recycling water vats, oil fails to separate from the slurry, causing weak spots and holes in newly rolled paper sheets.
Caution:
- Never put cheese or grease-soaked cardboard into the dry recycling bin.
Regional Notes:
- Check your local council rules, as many modern FOGO processing plants accept all unlined pizza boxes.

[Office Paper, Envelopes, Newspapers & Magazines]
Category: Recyclable
Disposal Instructions:
- Place loose office paper, notebook pages, envelopes, and newsprint directly into the paper recycling bin.
- Remove large plastic spiral bindings and plastic document covers; small metal staples and envelope plastic address windows are acceptable as they are filtered during pulping.
- Keep paper flat or lightly folded; avoid shredding paper unless necessary for confidential data.
Material & Recycling Context:
- High-grade white office paper contains virgin chemical pulp that is recycled into printing paper and facial tissues.
- Shredded paper fibers become too short to weave into strong paper and frequently slip through facility screen filters.
Caution:
- Waxed paper, parchment baking paper, and carbon receipts are not recyclable in the paper bin.
Regional Notes:
- If you have shredded paper, place it inside an unsoiled paper grocery bag or compost it at home.

[Beverage Cartons, Liquid Paperboard & Tetra Pak]
Category: Recyclable / Specialized Drop-off
Disposal Instructions:
- Empty milk, juice, or plant-based beverage cartons completely.
- Push the straw back into juice boxes or keep plastic screw caps attached.
- Flatten carton and place in the commingled recycling bin if accepted by your local municipality, or take to a CDS depot.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Liquid paperboard cartons consist of multi-layer laminates: approximately 75% paperboard, 20% polyethylene, and 5% aluminum barrier foil.
- Specialized hydropulping mills separate paper fibers for building insulation and ceiling tiles, while recovering plastic and aluminum (PolyAl).
Caution:
- Do not soak cartons in water prior to collection.
Regional Notes:
- In several regions, beverage cartons earn a 10-cent container deposit refund at authorized recycling drop points.

[Glass Bottles, Beverage Jars & Wine Bottles]
Category: Recyclable
Disposal Instructions:
- Empty all liquid or food sauce residue and give a brief rinse with cold water.
- Metal bottle caps and jar lids can be removed and dropped into metal recycling, or screwed back onto the clean glass bottle.
- Place glass containers directly into your designated glass-only purple bin or mixed recycling bin.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Container glass (soda-lime silica) is 100% recyclable infinitely without loss of quality, strength, or clarity.
- Recycled crushed glass cullet melts at lower furnace temperatures, saving significant energy and reducing industrial carbon dioxide emissions.
Caution:
- Broken glass should be wrapped safely in newspaper and placed in general waste to protect sanitation collection workers.
Regional Notes:
- Do not mix drinking cups, ceramics, or mirrors with bottle glass due to incompatible melting temperatures.

[Ceramics, Pyrex, Window Panes & Drinking Glassware]
Category: General waste / Specialized Masonry Drop-off
Disposal Instructions:
- Wrap broken crockery, ceramic mugs, porcelain plates, Pyrex dishes, and drinking tumblers in newspaper.
- Place securely into your red-lid general waste rubbish bin.
- Intact crockery and glassware in good usable condition should be donated to charitable thrift stores.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Borosilicate glass (Pyrex), lead crystal, and ceramics have significantly higher melting temperatures than container bottle glass.
- A single ceramic shard in a batch of molten bottle glass forms an unmelted inclusion that causes bottles to fracture under pressure during bottling.
Caution:
- Never dispose of ovenware, light fittings, or window glass in curbside glass recycling bins.
Regional Notes:
- Large quantities of architectural window glass can be accepted at dedicated building salvagers.

[Aluminum Beverage Cans, Clean Foil & Baking Trays]
Category: Recyclable
Disposal Instructions:
- Empty beverage cans completely; do not insert cigarette butts, tissues, or trash inside the can.
- For clean aluminum cooking foil, pie dishes, and chocolate wrappers, roll and crunch them together into a solid fist-sized ball.
- Place loose cans and foil balls directly into the commingled recycling bin.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Aluminum is 100% recyclable infinitely.
- Recycling aluminum requires 95% less energy and produces 95% fewer emissions compared to refining raw bauxite ore into new metal.
- Individual small pieces of foil get blown into paper sorting lines; balling them together ensures eddy-current sorters detect them.
Caution:
- Foil coated in thick cheese, barbecue grease, or baked-on crust that cannot be wiped clean belongs in general waste.
Regional Notes:
- Beverage cans are eligible for cash refunds at Container Deposit Scheme depots.

[Steel Food Tins, Aerosol Cans & Metal Paint Cans]
Category: Recyclable / Chemical Drop-off
Disposal Instructions:
- Rinse food soup, pet food, and tomato paste cans clean of residue.
- Place the metal lid inside the tin and squeeze the top of the can slightly to keep the sharp lid contained inside.
- Aerosol cans (deodorant, whipped cream, hairspray) must be completely emptied of propellant and pressure before curbside recycling.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Steel and tin-plated iron are magnetic metals sorted efficiently with overhead electro-magnets at materials recovery facilities.
- Recycled steel is fed into electric arc furnaces to forge steel beams, automotive frames, and household appliances.
Caution:
- Aerosols containing pesticide, spray paint, or solvent residue, or aerosols that still hiss under pressure, must go to household hazardous waste depots.
Regional Notes:
- Empty, dry metal paint cans with cured dried paint residue can be accepted at metal scrap yards or municipal collection stations.

[Household Lithium-Ion & Rechargeable Batteries]
Category: E-waste / Hazardous
Disposal Instructions:
- Never place any battery in standard household rubbish or curbside recycling bins.
- Place clear insulating tape or electrical tape across the positive and negative terminals of lithium, button-cell, and 9V batteries.
- Deposit taped batteries into dedicated battery drop-off bins located at supermarkets, hardware stores, and electronics retailers.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Modern electronics rely on lithium cobalt oxide, nickel-manganese-cobalt, or lithium iron phosphate chemistries.
- Specialized recyclers dismantle cells to extract high-purity lithium, cobalt, nickel, and copper for manufacturing new battery cells.
Caution:
- Punctured, crushed, or short-circuited lithium batteries experience thermal runaway fires that endanger waste collection trucks and sorting facilities.
Regional Notes:
- B-cycle and national battery stewardship schemes provide thousands of free neighborhood drop-off points nationwide.

[Single-Use Alkaline & Zinc-Carbon Batteries]
Category: E-waste / Hazardous
Disposal Instructions:
- Do not dispose of AA, AAA, C, D, or 9V alkaline batteries in the household rubbish bin.
- Store depleted alkaline batteries in a dry plastic container out of direct heat and away from children.
- Tape terminals on 9V batteries to prevent residual current loops.
- Drop off at community battery recycling collection units in grocery stores or civic waste stations.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Alkaline batteries contain zinc, manganese dioxide, steel, and potassium hydroxide electrolyte.
- Hydrometallurgical processing reclaims zinc and manganese compounds for reuse in agricultural micronutrient fertilizers and industrial steel manufacturing.
Caution:
- Leaking alkaline batteries should be handled with gloves; place leaking units inside a sealed clear ziplock bag before drop-off.
Regional Notes:
- Free drop-off bins are hosted in major grocery chains including ALDI, Woolworths, and Coles.

[Automotive Lead-Acid Batteries]
Category: Hazardous / Automotive Drop-off
Disposal Instructions:
- Keep car, motorcycle, and marine boat batteries upright at all times to prevent acidic electrolyte spills.
- Never place vehicle batteries into curbside bins or general waste skips.
- Transport to an auto parts retailer, mechanic workshop, scrap metal merchant, or council transfer station.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Lead-acid batteries achieve a recovery rate over 98% in closed-loop industrial recycling.
- Battery cases (polypropylene) are melted into new cases, sulfuric acid is neutralized into sodium sulfate for detergents, and lead is smelted into pure ingots.
Caution:
- Batteries contain concentrated sulfuric acid that causes chemical burns and lead that is a potent neurotoxin.
Regional Notes:
- Most automotive parts retailers offer free battery take-back, and scrap metal yards often pay cash for old car batteries.

[Smartphones, Tablets & Mobile Electronics]
Category: E-waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Back up personal photographs and documents to cloud storage or an external hard drive.
- Perform a complete factory reset and remove SIM cards, memory cards, and security passcodes.
- Bring the mobile phone, tablet, accessories, and charging cables to an authorized e-waste drop-off point or MobileMuster kiosk.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Circuit boards contain precious metals including gold, silver, palladium, and copper, as well as critical rare earth elements in vibration motors and speakers.
- Recycling 50,000 mobile phones recovers approximately 1 kilogram of gold and 350 kilograms of copper, avoiding raw mining extraction.
Caution:
- Never throw smart devices into red rubbish bins; their lithium pouches are easily pierced by garbage truck compactors.
Regional Notes:
- MobileMuster collection units accept phones, smartwatches, modems, landlines, and tablet computers at post offices and telecommunication shops.

[Laptops, Desktop Computers, Keyboards & Mice]
Category: E-waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Back up data and securely wipe the storage drive using a disk sanitization utility, or physically remove the drive for destruction.
- Coil cables and peripheral wires alongside computer keyboards, mice, and webcams.
- Take computer hardware to a National Television and Computer Recycling Scheme (NTCRS) drop-off depot or designated electronics retailer.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Computers contain a diverse mixture of engineering plastics (ABS-PC), aluminum heat sinks, copper wiring, and multi-layer circuit boards.
- Modern shredding and sorting lines use magnetic, air-knife, and optical sorting to separate plastics, ferrous metals, and non-ferrous metals.
Caution:
- Avoid cracking or breaking laptop display screens, as older models may contain mercury cold-cathode lamps.
Regional Notes:
- Major hardware chains and office supply retailers offer free e-waste recycling kiosks for small computer peripherals.

[Televisions, Computer Monitors & Flat Panels]
Category: E-waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Keep screens intact without cracking or shattering glass panels.
- Do not cut power cords off the units, as intact equipment is safer for recycling facility handlers.
- Deliver flat-panel LED, OLED, and LCD televisions and computer monitors to an NTCRS depot or council e-waste event.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Flat panel displays contain circuit driver chips, high-grade optical acrylic diffuser sheets, aluminum frames, and circuitry.
- Facilities dismantle panels into steel backplates, copper circuit boards, and clean plastics for granulating.
Caution:
- Older CRT (cathode-ray tube) televisions contain up to 2 kilograms of lead within the funnel glass and must be handled with care.
Regional Notes:
- Free drop-off is provided for households and small businesses under government-supported stewardship initiatives.

[Power Cables, Phone Chargers & Electronic Adapters]
Category: E-waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Bundle cords and power plugs together; secure loosely with a rubber band or twist tie.
- Do not put electrical cables into curbside recycling bins; wires tangle around spinning sorting shafts, causing machinery jams.
- Drop into e-waste recycling bins at civic transfer stations, electronics stores, or office supply stores.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Power cables contain high-purity electrical copper conductors surrounded by PVC or thermoplastic elastomer insulation.
- Mechanical wire strippers and granulators cleanly separate copper granules from outer plastic jacket polymers.
Caution:
- Damaged, frayed, or scorched cables should be taken out of home service immediately to prevent electrical fires.
Regional Notes:
- Office supply stores such as Officeworks provide dedicated cable recycling collection boxes.

[Small Kitchen Appliances: Toasters, Kettles & Blenders]
Category: E-waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Unplug appliance and clean out breadcrumbs, liquid remnants, or food residue.
- Wrap the power cord around the base of the appliance.
- Take small electrical appliances to designated small-appliance collection bins at council waste centers or participating electrical retailers.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Small household appliances contain electric heating elements, copper-wound electric motors, stainless steel housings, and heat-resistant plastics.
- Shredders fragment whole units, followed by magnetic separators to recover steel and eddy-current separators for non-ferrous metals.
Caution:
- Never throw electrical appliances into curbside yellow recycling or red rubbish bins.
Regional Notes:
- Working appliances in clean cosmetic condition can be donated to community reuse centers or repair cafes.

[Fluorescent Light Tubes & Compact Fluorescent Lamps (CFLs)]
Category: Hazardous / E-waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Handle carefully by the base to prevent cracking or shattering the glass tube.
- Never place fluorescent tubes or CFL globes in curbside domestic rubbish or recycling bins.
- Place in original cardboard packaging or wrap safely in bubble wrap, and take to a hazardous waste or light globe recycling point.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Fluorescent lamps contain phosphorus powder and small quantities of elemental mercury vapor.
- Distillation chambers use vacuum heat recovery to vaporize and capture pure liquid mercury, while separating clean glass and aluminum end caps.
Caution:
- If a fluorescent tube breaks indoors, ventilate the room for 15 minutes, avoid vacuuming, and scoop debris with cardboard into a sealed jar.
Regional Notes:
- Hardware retailers including Bunnings host free light globe and tube collection units in customer entry areas.

[LED Light Bulbs & Incandescent Bulbs]
Category: E-waste / General waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Traditional incandescent and halogen bulbs contain non-hazardous tungsten filaments and should be wrapped in paper and placed in general waste.
- Modern LED light bulbs contain electronic driver circuit boards and aluminum heat sinks; they should be taken to an e-waste or lighting drop-off point.
- Do not place any light bulb into household curbside glass recycling bins.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Light globe glass has different thermal properties than container bottles and will ruin bottle recycling melts.
- LED bulbs contain valuable microchips, heat-sink aluminum, and copper wiring that recyclers process alongside other electronics.
Caution:
- Ensure globes are cool to the touch before unscrewing or handling for disposal.
Regional Notes:
- Many supermarkets and hardware chains accept all types of household light globes for recycling.

[Kitchen Vegetable Peelings, Fruit Cores & Plant Scraps]
Category: Organic / Compost
Disposal Instructions:
- Collect carrot peelings, apple cores, potato skins, cabbage leaves, and fruit trimmings in a kitchen caddy.
- Line the caddy with newspaper or a certified compostable liner (look for AS 4736 seedling or AS 5810 home compostable logos).
- Empty contents into your green-lid FOGO organics bin or your backyard home compost bin.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Food scraps are rich in nitrogen, water, and organic matter.
- In commercial composting facilities, aerobic digestion pasteurizes organic waste at temperatures above 55 degrees Celsius, creating nutrient-dense soil compost.
- Diverting organics from landfills prevents anaerobic decomposition that generates methane, a greenhouse gas 28 times more potent than carbon dioxide.
Caution:
- Remove all plastic price-look-up (PLU) stickers and rubber bands from fruit before discarding.
Regional Notes:
- Check your local council guidelines, as modern FOGO collection services are available across many municipal areas.

[Cooked Food Scraps, Meat Scraps & Bones]
Category: Organic / General waste
Disposal Instructions:
- If your municipality provides a FOGO service that accepts all food organics, place cooked leftovers, meat scraps, poultry bones, and seafood shells into the green bin.
- If your council green bin is for garden prunings only, or if you only have a standard home compost bin, place meat scraps and bones into the red general waste bin.
- Freeze meat and seafood scraps in a small container until collection day to prevent odors and flies in summer.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Commercial industrial composting facilities reach sustained high temperatures that break down animal proteins, fats, and pathogens completely.
- Home compost bins usually do not achieve adequate temperatures to process meat and bones quickly, which can attract vermin and pests.
Caution:
- Never put plastic food packaging, cling wrap, or Styrofoam trays into organic bins.
Regional Notes:
- Always check council FOGO literature to verify whether meat, dairy, and bones are permitted in your specific area.

[Coffee Grounds, Unbleached Filters & Loose Tea Leaves]
Category: Organic / Compost
Disposal Instructions:
- Empty espresso coffee pucks, loose drip grounds, unbleached paper coffee filters, and loose tea leaves directly into your kitchen caddy or home compost.
- Coffee grounds can also be sprinkled sparingly and directly onto garden soil around acid-loving plants such as azaleas, camellias, and blueberries.
- For single-use coffee pods, check if they are certified compostable; aluminum pods belong in specialized brand mail-back drop-off programs.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Coffee grounds provide valuable organic nitrogen (approx 2% nitrogen by volume), carbon, potassium, and phosphorus.
- Earthworms thrive on coffee grounds in worm farms, accelerating the generation of rich vermicompost castings.
Caution:
- Conventional pyramid tea bags frequently contain plastic polyethylene or polypropylene fibers that do not break down in compost.
Regional Notes:
- Standard aluminum or plastic coffee capsules must not go into curbside recycling bins loose.

[Certified Compostable Bags & Bioplastic Packaging]
Category: Organic / General waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Check certification markings: look for the seedling logo with AS 4736 (commercial compostable) or AS 5810 (home compostable).
- Use AS 4736 bags as kitchen caddy bin liners for municipal green FOGO bins where approved by your council.
- If a bioplastic product is marked merely degradable or oxo-degradable without certification, place it into the general waste red bin.
- Do not place any compostable bags or bioplastics into curbside yellow recycling bins.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Certified compostable materials are manufactured from plant starches (corn starch, potato starch) or bio-polyesters like PBAT.
- These materials disintegrate within 12 weeks and biodegrade completely into water, carbon dioxide, and biomass under industrial composting conditions.
Caution:
- Oxo-degradable plastics simply fragment into millions of microplastics and cause severe ecological harm.
Regional Notes:
- Several councils supply free rolls of certified green FOGO caddy liners to residents annually.

[Garden Waste, Lawn Clippings, Leaves & Tree Prunings]
Category: Organic / Compost
Disposal Instructions:
- Place cut grass, raked autumn leaves, weeds, small branches, and shrub prunings into your green-lid garden organics bin.
- Prune long branches so they fit comfortably within the bin with the lid completely closed; branches should not exceed 10 centimeters in diameter.
- Keep organic matter loose inside the bin; do not bind branches with wire or plastic twine.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Garden organics are shredded, screened, blended with carbon mulch, and composted according to Australian Standard AS 4454.
- High-grade mulch and soil conditioners are supplied back to local agriculture, parks, and home gardeners.
Caution:
- Do not place treated timber, fence palings, large tree stumps, dirt, rocks, or synthetic turf into garden bins.
Regional Notes:
- Many civic waste management facilities accept trailer loads of clean green garden waste for reduced tipping fees.

[Used Cooking Oil & Deep Fryer Grease]
Category: Specialized Drop-off / General waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Allow hot cooking oil to cool completely inside the pan or fryer.
- For small domestic amounts (under 200 ml), absorb with paper towels and place into general waste, or compost if grease-free.
- For larger quantities, pour into an empty screw-cap plastic bottle or metal can and take to a council recycling transfer station.
- Never pour cooking oil, liquid grease, or melted fat down household kitchen sinks or drains.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Recovered waste cooking oil is filtered, dewatered, and chemically transesterified into clean-burning renewable biodiesel and bio-lubricants.
- Biodiesel from recycled vegetable oil produces up to 85% fewer greenhouse gas emissions compared to fossil diesel.
Caution:
- Pouring oil down drains creates thick fatbergs that block municipal sewer pipes and cause costly sewage overflows.
Regional Notes:
- Most council resource recovery centers accept up to 20 liters of household cooking oil for free.

[Expired Prescription Drugs & Over-The-Counter Medicines]
Category: Hazardous / Pharmacy Return
Disposal Instructions:
- Collect expired tablets, blister packs, liquid medications, ointments, and medicated creams.
- Take all unwanted medicines to any community retail pharmacy and hand them to the pharmacist.
- Pharmacists place them into the RUM (Return Unwanted Medicines) collection project bins for safe destruction.
- Outer cardboard medication boxes can be recycled with paper.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Pharmaceutical waste collected through RUM is transported to high-temperature incinerators certified by the EPA.
- High-temperature destruction completely breaks down complex active chemical molecules into inert ash.
Caution:
- Never flush medications down toilets or rinse down sink drains, as wastewater treatment plants cannot filter pharmaceutical compounds from rivers and oceans.
Regional Notes:
- The Return Unwanted Medicines program is free and funded across all Australian community pharmacies.

[Medical Sharps, Hypodermic Needles & Lancets]
Category: Hazardous / Sharps Collection
Disposal Instructions:
- Immediately after use, place needles, insulin syringes, lancets, and infusion sets into a rigid, puncture-proof yellow sharps container (AS 4031 compliant).
- Never recap, bend, or snap needles with your bare hands.
- Keep sharps containers out of reach of children.
- Take full sharps containers to a hospital exchange program, designated council drop point, or participating community pharmacy.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Sharps containers are sealed, tracked, and treated via commercial medical autoclaves (high-pressure steam sterilization) or dedicated medical incinerators.
Caution:
- Never dispose of loose sharps or filled containers into household red, yellow, or green curbside bins; sharps pose life-threatening stick injuries to sanitation staff.
Regional Notes:
- Needle and Syringe Programs (NSP) provide free sterile equipment and replacement sharps containers.

[Liquid Paints, Varnishes & Wood Stains]
Category: Hazardous / Paintback Drop-off
Disposal Instructions:
- Never pour liquid paints, enamels, or stains down sinks, street gutters, or stormwater drains.
- Do not place liquid paint cans into domestic general waste or recycling bins.
- Transport cans containing wet paint to an authorized Paintback drop-off center or municipal chemical drop event.
- If a paint tin contains only a dry skim of hardened water-based acrylic, leave the lid off until fully dry, then place the dry can in general waste.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Paintback processes collected paint by separating water and acrylic solvents; waste solvent paint is converted into industrial alternative fuels.
- Steel paint containers are cleaned and recycled into new steel products.
Caution:
- Oil-based paints contain volatile organic compounds (VOCs) that are flammable and toxic to inhale in unventilated areas.
Regional Notes:
- Paintback accepts up to 100 liters of trade and domestic architectural paint per visit at participating locations for free.

[Mineral Turpentine, Paint Thinners & Solvents]
Category: Hazardous
Disposal Instructions:
- Store solvents in their original, tightly closed containers with original warning labels intact.
- Keep away from heat sources, open flames, and direct sunlight.
- Deliver unwanted chemical solvents to a council household chemical cleanout program (e.g., Chemical CleanOut or Detox Your Home).
Material & Recycling Context:
- Industrial solvent recyclers use fractional distillation to separate pure thinners and cleaning solvents from dissolved paint resins for reuse.
Caution:
- Highly flammable and dangerous vapors; never pour solvents into drains, soil, or waterways.
Regional Notes:
- Chemical cleanup events take place periodically and are free for household chemical disposal.

[Pesticides, Weedkillers & Garden Fungicides]
Category: Hazardous
Disposal Instructions:
- Keep all concentrated garden chemicals, weedkillers, and insecticides in their clearly marked original packaging.
- Never mix different chemical containers together.
- Deliver unwanted garden chemicals to municipal household hazardous waste collection facilities.
- For commercial agricultural chemical drums, participate in the drumMUSTER collection program after triple-rinsing.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Agricultural chemicals undergo high-temperature thermal oxidation to eliminate active toxins safely without producing hazardous residues.
Caution:
- Even minor traces of chemical runoff from washing spray equipment can poison aquatic ecosystems and urban waterways.
Regional Notes:
- drumMUSTER provides free collection points across regional agricultural centers for eligible cleaned chemical drums.

[Household Bleach, Ammonia & Caustic Drain Cleaners]
Category: Hazardous / General waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Use up remaining cleaning products according to label directions whenever possible.
- If disposing of leftover concentrated solutions, take to a hazardous chemical collection point.
- For empty containers: rinse thoroughly with water, replace the plastic cap, and place the clean plastic bottle into curbside recycling.
Material & Recycling Context:
- High-strength corrosive chemicals are treated in industrial wastewater facilities through controlled neutralization before discharge.
Caution:
- Never mix chlorine bleach with ammonia or acidic cleaners; mixing produces deadly toxic chloramine and chlorine gases instantly.
Regional Notes:
- Check municipal chemical cleanout schedules for safe disposal of bulk household cleaning supplies.

[Used Engine Motor Oil & Car Fluids]
Category: Hazardous / Automotive Drop-off
Disposal Instructions:
- Drain used motor oil, transmission fluid, and differential gear oil into a clean, leak-proof plastic or metal container.
- Do not mix motor oil with brake fluid, antifreeze coolant, water, or solvents, as cross-contamination prevents re-refining.
- Deliver used oil to an automotive workshop, service station, or council oil recovery depot.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Used motor oil does not wear out; it merely collects dirt and oxidation combustion products.
- Re-refining facilities use vacuum distillation and hydrotreating to purify used oil back into virgin-grade base lubricating oil repeatedly.
- Recycling just 4 liters of used engine oil produces enough clean base lubricating oil for an entire automobile oil change.
Caution:
- Discharging motor oil onto the ground or down storm drains is illegal and severely contaminates groundwater aquifers.
Regional Notes:
- Most council waste management facilities feature dedicated oil collection tanks where residents can deposit used engine oil for free.

[Clothing, Wearable Apparel & Clean Footwear]
Category: Donation / Textile Recycling / General waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Clean and wash wearable shirts, pants, jackets, and shoes.
- Tie matching shoes together by their laces so pairs are not separated during sorting.
- Deliver clean wearable garments to charity op-shops, clothing donation bins, or community thrift stores.
- Severely worn, torn, or stained clothes should be taken to dedicated textile recycling drop points or cut into household cleaning rags.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Wearable donated clothing directly extends garment life and supports non-profit charitable social programs.
- Unwearable cottons and synthetics collected by textile recyclers are mechanically shredded into shoddy fibers for vehicle insulation and carpet underlay.
Caution:
- Do not place any clothes, blankets, or shoes into curbside yellow recycling bins; textiles tangle conveyor rollers and break plant sorting machinery.
Regional Notes:
- Brands such as Zara, H&M, and retail department stores host in-store garment take-back bins for all condition textiles.

[Torn Fabrics, Rags & Damaged Bedding]
Category: Textile Recycling / General waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Cut unwearable cotton shirts and towels into absorbent cleaning rags for workshops, garages, or home cleaning.
- If textile recycling kiosks are available nearby, drop off clean worn rags for mechanical fiber recovery.
- If no textile drop-off or reuse opportunity exists, place worn rags into your general waste red bin.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Textile shredding lines shred natural and synthetic fabrics into post-consumer filler fibers used in acoustic insulation, moving blankets, and mattress pads.
Caution:
- Rags soaked in linseed oil or flammable solvents can undergo spontaneous combustion if bunched up; lay flat to dry outdoors before disposal.
Regional Notes:
- Check local municipal resource centers for textile drop-off programs that accept stained or damaged fabric.

[Mattresses, Box Springs & Foam Bedding]
Category: Bulky Waste / Specialized Drop-off
Disposal Instructions:
- Schedule a council bulky waste household collection service, or deliver directly to a resource recovery transfer station.
- Keep the mattress clean and dry prior to pickup; waterlogged mattresses are heavy and prone to mold, preventing material recovery.
- If purchasing a replacement mattress, check if the retail delivery service includes removal and recycling of your old mattress.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Dedicated mattress recyclers slit the outer fabric to separate polyurethane foam, steel innerspring coils, timber frames, and cotton wadding.
- Over 75% of a mattress by weight is recovered: steel springs are melted into new metal products, and foam is granulated for carpet underlayment.
Caution:
- Never dump mattresses in bushland or public areas; illegal dumping carries heavy fines.
Regional Notes:
- Soft Landing operates social enterprise mattress recycling schemes across multiple states and councils.

[Automobile Tires & Bicycle Rubber Tubes]
Category: Specialized Drop-off / Automotive
Disposal Instructions:
- Never place car, motorcycle, or bicycle tires into household red or yellow curbside bins.
- Leave worn tires with the tire retailer when fitting replacements, or deliver to a licensed council transfer station.
- Bicycle inner tubes can often be returned to local community bicycle shops participating in rubber recycling schemes.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Tire Stewardship programs recover worn rubber by shredding tires into crumb rubber, granules, and steel bead wire.
- Crumb rubber is vulcanized into playground safety matting, athletic running tracks, brake pads, and rubberized road asphalt.
Caution:
- Stockpiling tires creates fire hazards with toxic smoke and provides breeding pools for disease-carrying mosquitoes.
Regional Notes:
- Retailers accredited by Tire Stewardship Australia safely recycle recovered consumer tires.

[Treated Timber, Pine Posts & CCA Wood]
Category: General waste / Demolition Drop-off
Disposal Instructions:
- Keep treated timber (copper chrome arsenate, creosote, or LOSP treated) out of green organic and composting bins.
- Place small pieces in the general waste red bin; transport large fence posts and decking to a council transfer station.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Copper Chrome Arsenate (CCA) timber is infused with chemical preservatives to resist rot and termites.
- CCA wood cannot be mulched into landscape compost or converted into particleboard due to hazardous arsenic and chromium content.
Caution:
- Never burn treated timber in home fireplaces, outdoor firepits, or barbecues; combustion releases lethal arsenic smoke and toxic ash.
Regional Notes:
- Transfer stations classify treated wood separately from clean untreated firewood or demolition timber.

[Bricks, Concrete, Ceramic Tiles & Masonry Rubble]
Category: Construction & Demolition Drop-off
Disposal Instructions:
- Do not place heavy rubble, masonry bricks, concrete pavers, or porcelain wall tiles into domestic red or yellow curbside bins.
- Overloading curbside bins can damage truck robotic lift arms.
- Take masonry waste to a specialized construction and demolition (C&D) recycling depot or hire a dedicated skip bin.
Material & Recycling Context:
- C&D facilities crush concrete, bricks, and tiles using jaw crushers and impactors, screening them into graded crushed aggregate and road base.
- Recycled road base aggregate replaces virgin quarried gravel in highway construction, civil paving, and drainage beds.
Caution:
- Structures built before 1990 may contain bonded asbestos fibro sheeting; never disturb or deliver suspect materials without licensed testing.
Regional Notes:
- Clean separated concrete is typically accepted at concrete recyclers for significantly lower tipping fees than mixed waste.

[Thermal Receipts & Carbon Copy Paper]
Category: General waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Place supermarket shopping receipts, parking tickets, and ATM receipts into the red-lid general waste bin.
- Do not put thermal cash register receipts into the paper recycling bin.
- Switch to digital, SMS, or email receipts where available at retail checkout counters.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Thermal receipt paper is coated with reactive chemical dyes and bisphenol compounds (BPA or BPS) that produce print when exposed to heat.
- Introducing thermal receipts into paper recycling slurry spreads endocrine-disrupting BPA into recycled paper towels, food packaging, and toilet tissue.
Caution:
- Do not shred thermal receipts into compost or worm farms.
Regional Notes:
- Keep all shiny thermal receipts out of yellow recycling bins.

[Disposable Baby Diapers, Nappies & Wet Wipes]
Category: General waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Roll soiled diapers tightly, fold tabs securely around the bundle, and place into a small bag before disposing into the general waste red bin.
- Keep all disposable nappies, adult incontinence pads, and sanitary napkins strictly out of recycling and green compost bins.
- Disposable wet wipes, makeup remover wipes, and disinfecting wipes must also be placed into general rubbish.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Conventional disposable diapers consist of multi-component superabsorbent polymer gels (sodium polyacrylate), polypropylene covers, and cellulose fluff.
- Soiled nappies in recycling streams pose biological biohazard risks to manual sorting personnel.
Caution:
- Never flush wet wipes down toilets, even if packaging claims flushable; wipes do not disintegrate and form catastrophic sewer pipe blockages.
Regional Notes:
- Some municipalities offer an additional red bin or specialized weekly nappy collection service for families with infants.

[Pet Waste, Cat Litter & Animal Bedding]
Category: General waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Collect canine dog droppings in compostable or standard waste bags and place into the red-lid general waste bin.
- Used clay, bentonite, silica gel, or paper-based cat litter must be bagged and discarded in the general waste bin.
- Guinea pig, rabbit, and rodent bedding (sawdust, straw) should be placed in general waste unless your council explicitly approves vegetarian pet bedding in FOGO.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Animal feces can carry dangerous pathogens, bacteria (Salmonella, E. coli), and parasites like Toxoplasma gondii.
- Municipal composting facilities often cannot ensure thermal destruction of Toxoplasma oocysts, which survive in finished compost and harm public health.
Caution:
- Never flush cat litter or pet feces down the toilet; municipal water treatment plants cannot eliminate Toxoplasma parasites, which threaten marine life.
Regional Notes:
- Dedicated home pet-waste worm digesters installed in backyards can treat canine waste separate from food gardens.

[Cigarette Butts, Ash & Disposable Lighters]
Category: General waste
Disposal Instructions:
- Ensure cigarette butts are fully extinguished in water or an ashtray before disposal.
- Place cold cigarette filters and tobacco ash into the red-lid general waste rubbish bin.
- Empty plastic pocket lighters must be completely exhausted of butane fuel before being placed into general waste.
Material & Recycling Context:
- Cigarette filters are not cotton or paper; they are manufactured from cellulose acetate, a non-biodegradable synthetic plastic fiber.
- Discarded butts leach heavy metals, arsenic, lead, and nicotine into waterways and soil, taking over a decade to fragment into microplastics.
Caution:
- Never toss burning cigarette butts into garden mulch, bushland, or out of vehicle windows; discarded butts ignite severe bushfires.
Regional Notes:
- Specialized TerraCycle mail-in boxes accept cigarette waste for plastic pelletizing and industrial composting of residual tobacco.
